\begin{abstract}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Problem: low-cost capacitive sensors are noisy, drift over time, and require calibration that most consumers will not perform.
  \item Goal: infer a mapping between raw sensor units and volumetric water content without manual calibration.
  \item Approach: a hidden-state time-series model with known watering events (added volume) and known pot volume, using multiple sensors per pot to average heterogeneity.
  \item Result preview: the model yields online estimates of moisture states and stable threshold bands for control/alerts.
  \item Impact: supports consumer-ready calibration-free deployments with ESP32-based systems.
  \item Tone: the technology has promise but persistent issues (drift, heterogeneity, and unobserved losses) limit naive deployments; we present a mitigation strategy rather than a guarantee.
\end{itemize}
\end{abstract}

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Model overview (bullet skeleton)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item State: true volumetric water content $x_t$ per pot; evolves with evapotranspiration and drainage.
  \item Inputs: watering events at times $t_k$ with known added volume $\Delta V_k$.
  \item Observations: $N$ capacitive sensors per pot producing raw readings $y_{i,t}$.
  \item Assumption: sensor responses are monotonic in $x_t$ with sensor-specific offsets and noise; heterogeneity modeled as Gaussian.
  \item Unknowns: mapping from raw units to moisture, sensor offsets, process noise, and drift.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Assumptions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Watering events are inferred from sensor time series and validated against manual observations.
  \item Soil volume is known or estimated with bounded error.
  \item The shared response curve $h(\\cdot)$ is monotonic over the operating range.
  \item Effective permittivity is approximately linear in water content over a limited range; nonlinear models (e.g., Topp/CRIM) are considered when justified.
  \item Sensor offsets are stationary over short windows and drift slowly over long windows.
  \item Within-pot heterogeneity is approximated by a Gaussian offset distribution.
  \item Evapotranspiration can be treated as a smooth, nonnegative process noise term.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Watering event inference}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Compute a smooth baseline using an exponential weighted moving average (EWMA) \cite{hunter1986-ewma}.
  \item Compute lagged differences and robust z-scores using a rolling median absolute deviation (MAD) scale \cite{huber1981-robust}.
  \item Trigger candidate events using a two-sided p-value threshold on the z-scores.
  \item Confirm candidates with a hysteresis test: within a window, require $k$ samples whose residual magnitude exceeds a threshold.
  \item Merge detections separated by less than a minimum time distance.
  \item Watering events are defined as putative events where the sensor reading decreases by at least a threshold; increases are excluded (TODO: formalize detection rule).
  \item Detected events are validated against manually logged watering observations; manual checks indicate high accuracy.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Software system overview}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Firmware reads two ADC channels on boot, advertises measurements via BLE, and enters deep sleep between advertising windows.
  \item A BLE monitor service listens for advertisements and forwards readings to the controller API.
  \item A lightweight HTTP controller exposes time-series queries and observation endpoints used by the inference pipeline.
  \item Database helpers store sensor readings and inferred events for later analysis and validation.
  \item Event inference runs as a scheduled job to append automatic observations to the system log.
  \item Soil context is tracked per pot in the database, and the response curve is updated online per pot as watering proceeds.
  \item A weighted evidence scheme can pool a global response curve across pots while refining per-pot curves using local measurements.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Derivation (basic)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Volume-averaged hidden state.} Let $Z_t(\mathbf{r})$ denote the local volumetric water content at position $\mathbf{r}$ in the pot volume $V$. The hidden state is the volume average:
  \begin{equation}
    \bar{Z}_t = \frac{1}{V} \int_V Z_t(\mathbf{r}) \, dV.
  \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Relative moisture state.} We also define a normalized state:
  \begin{equation}
    Q_t = \frac{\bar{Z}_t - Z_{\min}}{Z_{\max} - Z_{\min}}.
  \end{equation}
  $Z_{\min}$ is a practical lower bound (often near zero) and $Z_{\max}$ represents a practical upper bound (e.g., observed post-watering maximum or field capacity). This aligns with real usage where thresholds are expressed as relative dryness rather than absolute VWC.
  \item \textbf{Operational bounds.} The corresponding sensor readings $X(Z_{\max})$ and $X(Z_{\min})$ can be obtained with minimal user effort: $Z_{\max}$ by fully wetting the soil and recording the sensor output, and $Z_{\min}$ is often approximated as zero when a true dry-down is impractical.
  \item \textbf{Sensor approximation.} With $N$ sensors at locations $\{\mathbf{r}_i\}_{i=1}^N$, a conventional quadrature-style approximation is
  \begin{equation}
    \bar{Z}_t \approx \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N Z_t(\mathbf{r}_i),
  \end{equation}
  which is adopted for convenience and is most reasonable when sensors sample the main spatial variability.
  \item \textbf{State dynamics.} We model the volume-average state with a discrete-time update:
  \begin{equation}
    \bar{Z}_{t+1} = \bar{Z}_t - \eta_t + \sum_k \delta_{t,t_k} \frac{\Delta V_k}{V_{soil}} + w_t,
  \end{equation}
  where $\eta_t \ge 0$ captures evapotranspiration and drainage, which depends on factors like current dryness, temperature, airflow, and drainage conditions. For simplicity we treat this loss as a constant $\eta$ in estimation, and absorb residual variability into $w_t$; $V_{soil}$ is the pot soil volume, $\delta_{t,t_k}$ is 1 at watering steps, $\Delta V_k$ is the known added water volume at event $k$, and $w_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_w^2)$.
  \item \textbf{Observation model.} Capacitive probes respond to effective permittivity, which increases with water content because liquid water has much higher permittivity than air or soil solids. This motivates a monotonic relationship between $Z_t$ and sensor output over typical operating ranges; deviations arise under salinity and frequency effects \cite{seyfried2004-50mhz,kelleners2005-frequency-dependence}. We treat $\varepsilon_{i,t}$ as a conceptual intermediate and model $X_{i,t}$ directly as $X_{i,t} = h(Z_t(\mathbf{r}_i)) + b_i + v_{i,t}$, where $h(\cdot)$ is an unknown monotonic mapping. The permittivity form is included only to connect to standard dielectric models such as Topp's equation and CRIM; it is not required for estimation.
  \begin{equation}
    \varepsilon_{i,t} \approx \alpha Z_t(\mathbf{r}_i) + \beta.
  \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Raw sensor units.} Each sensor reports a raw unit $X_{i,t}$ that is a monotonic transform of $\varepsilon_{i,t}$:
  \begin{equation}
    X_{i,t} = h(\varepsilon_{i,t}) + b_i + v_{i,t}, \quad v_{i,t} \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_v^2),
  \end{equation}
  where $h(\cdot)$ is a shared monotonic response curve (for v2.0.0 hardware), and $b_i$ is a sensor-specific offset capturing heterogeneity. This yields a composite mapping $h(\alpha Z + \beta)$ with unknown parameters.
  \item \textbf{Heterogeneity.} We model offsets as $b_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_b^2)$, giving a Gaussian spread across the $N$ sensors and supporting averaging to reduce bias from local soil pockets.
  \item \textbf{Online identification.} We estimate $\bar{Z}_t$, $b_i$, and a parametric form of $h$ (e.g., piecewise-linear or low-order polynomial) by maximizing the likelihood of observed $X_{i,t}$ over time with known inputs $\Delta V_k$. Given sufficient watering events, the state increments from $\Delta V_k / V_{soil}$ anchor the scale of the $h(\alpha Z + \beta)$ mapping.
  \item \textbf{Delta calibration.} For a watering event at time $t_k$ with known volume $\Delta V_k$, the model implies a known increment in the hidden state:
  \begin{equation}
    \Delta \bar{Z}_{t_k} = \frac{\Delta V_k}{V_{soil}}.
  \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Normalized delta.} The corresponding increment in the normalized state is
  \begin{equation}
    \Delta Q_{t_k} = \frac{\Delta \bar{Z}_{t_k}}{Z_{\max} - Z_{\min}},
  \end{equation}
  which can be used directly when thresholds are defined in relative units.
  \item \textbf{Measured delta.} The corresponding change in measured units can be summarized by the per-pot average
  \begin{equation}
    \Delta \bar{X}_{t_k} \approx \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \left( X_{i,t_k^+} - X_{i,t_k^-} \right).
  \end{equation}
  Each pair $(\Delta \bar{Z}_{t_k}, \Delta \bar{X}_{t_k})$ provides a local slope constraint on the response curve, which can be accumulated to fit the parameterized mapping from $Z$ to $X$ and to estimate $\bar{Z}_t$ online.
  \item \textbf{Problem definition (inference form).} Given per-pot averages $\bar{X}_t$, known watering increments $\Delta \bar{Z}_{t_k} = \Delta V_k / V_{soil}$, and endpoint measurements $X(Z_{\min})$ and $X(Z_{\max})$, infer a monotonic mapping between $\bar{X}_t$ and $Z_t$ (or $Q_t$) that is consistent with these constraints. The task reduces to estimating $Z_t \approx Z(\bar{X}_t) + \epsilon_t$ while fitting a constrained response curve that respects both endpoint bounds and observed delta responses.
  \item \textbf{Model class and estimation.} This formulation is a nonlinear state-space model (continuous-state hidden Markov model) with an unobserved state $\bar{Z}_t$ and noisy observations $X_{i,t}$. For estimation, we propose an extended Kalman filter (EKF) for online state tracking combined with maximum-likelihood parameter fitting of $h$ and offsets $b_i$ via expectation-maximization (EM) \cite{sarkka2013-bayesian}. For stronger nonlinearities, a particle filter can replace the EKF while keeping the same likelihood-based parameter updates.
  \item \textbf{Assessment with limited ground truth.} If a small number of direct $Z(t)$ measurements are available (e.g., gravimetric samples), we use them only for model assessment: to quantify bias, drift, and error bounds, not to fit the mapping.
  \item \textbf{Threshold inference.} Once $h$ is estimated, we map raw units to VWC and define thresholds (e.g., irrigation triggers) as quantiles of $\bar{Z}_t$ or domain-specific bands.
  \item \textbf{Uncertainty estimates.} To quantify uncertainty on the inferred response curve, we propose bootstrap resampling of watering events and sensor data with re-fit monotone regression, yielding empirical confidence bands on $Q(X)$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Validation}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Compare inferred watering events against manual watering logs to estimate event detection accuracy.
  \item Perform limited gravimetric or reference measurements of $Z(t)$ to assess bias and drift without fitting the model.
  \item Evaluate sensitivity of the inferred mapping to soil volume error and watering volume uncertainty.
  \item Check stability of $h(\cdot)$ and offsets across time windows to quantify drift.
  \item Report failure cases where heterogeneity, salinity, or temperature effects break the inferred mapping.
\end{itemize}

\section{Results (expected)}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Simulation with synthetic pots shows convergence of $h$ and $b_i$ after $M$ watering events.
  \item Multi-sensor averaging reduces variance in estimated VWC versus single-sensor baselines.
  \item Online thresholds stabilize within practical error bounds for consumer plant-care.
  \item v2.0.0 sensor behavior: monotonic curve with device-specific offset and mild drift.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Practical deployment: need for accurate watering volume logs (user input or flow sensor).
  \item Soil-volume estimation error propagates directly into calibration scale.
  \item Sensor placement and pot geometry limit the assumption of Gaussian heterogeneity.
  \item Drift and temperature sensitivity may require periodic re-identification.
  \item Failure modes: blocked drainage, uneven watering, or root growth changing soil structure.
  \item Prior calibration studies indicate that sensor-to-sensor variability can be large, making universal curves unreliable without per-sensor reference checks \cite{bogena2017-calibration}.
  \item Soil-type and temperature effects observed in field calibration studies can dominate errors, emphasizing the need for soil-specific validation \cite{merlin2007-calibration}.
  \item Measurement frequency effects in EM sensing constrain generalizability across sensors and soils \cite{sheng2025-frequency,kelleners2005-frequency-dependence}.
  \item Low-cost capacitance sensors can show sensitivity to conductivity and temperature, which must be modeled or bounded in practice \cite{kizito2008-frequency}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Flaws and limitations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The method can be under-identified if watering events are too infrequent or too small.
  \item Shared $g(\\cdot)$ may be invalid across sensor batches or under salinity changes.
  \item Model ignores hysteresis and drying/rewetting asymmetry common in porous media.
  \item Evapotranspiration and drainage are unobserved; errors can masquerade as calibration drift.
  \item Multi-sensor averaging can hide localized dry zones that matter for root health.
  \item Online thresholds may lag fast changes, leading to delayed irrigation decisions.
\end{itemize}

\section{Scientific limitations and pitfalls}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Identifiability can fail when watering events are sparse or too small to provide informative signal.
  \item Model mismatch: capacitive response is nonlinear and hysteretic; a single monotonic curve can be wrong.
  \item Unobserved losses (evapotranspiration, drainage) can be misattributed to calibration drift.
  \item Input uncertainty in watering volume or soil volume directly biases the inferred mapping.
  \item Spatial heterogeneity may be non-Gaussian or layered, breaking the volume-average approximation.
  \item Drift and temperature sensitivity violate stationarity of sensor offsets over time.
  \item Identifiability depends on informative watering events; sparse or uniform events slow convergence.
  \item Nonlinearity and hysteresis in capacitive sensors violate a single shared $g(\cdot)$.
  \item Assumed Gaussian heterogeneity may under-model heavy-tailed or layered soils.
  \item Confounding from salinity and fertilizer changes; sensor response may shift without water change.
  \item Evapotranspiration term $\eta_t$ is unobserved and can alias with calibration drift.
  \item Pot-scale dynamics may be non-Markov due to preferential flow and water channeling.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Measurement-free online calibration is plausible with known inputs and multi-sensor redundancy.
  \item ESP32 platforms enable low-cost data logging and on-device inference.
\end{itemize}
